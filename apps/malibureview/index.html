Four steps

From a cut on disk to notes in your edit.

01

Play the cut

Open the video. The start timecode comes from the file, drop-frame is supported, and you move with JKL, frame steps and an A-B loop.

02

Drop markers and notes

Mark a frame or a range, pick a type, write the note. Reply to markers and tick them off when they are done.

03

Export what you found

Send the markers out as FCPXML, EDL, Premiere CSV or PDF, or drag them straight to Final Cut Pro.

04

Follow along in Final Cut Pro

With Final Cut Pro installed, the playhead stays in sync with the review window.

What's inside

What MalibuReview does today, as tested internally.

Playback

Frame-accurate, with the timecode you expect

Playback with JKL shuttle, frame steps and an A-B loop. The start timecode is read from the file, and drop-frame is supported.

Markers with substance

8 built-in marker types plus custom types, range markers with in and out, replies and a done flag, and filters. Everything is stored locally in a sidecar file.

Export to where the notes go

FCPXML (as markers or as titles), EDL, Premiere CSV, PDF and Frame.io comments. Plus “Drag to FCP” and a single-frame PNG.

Final Cut Pro integration

Import .fcpxml, .fcpxmld and media. A share destination installer and a workflow extension for playhead sync. Needs Final Cut Pro installed.

Compare mode “Gang”

Up to 4 videos in a grid, played in sync on one Mac.

Audio tools

Meters for up to 8 channels, peak and RMS. Choose the audio track, and set an audio offset.

Burned-in watermark export

Export a video with name, IP address and timestamp burned in, via the File menu. Export only: there is no live overlay.

iPhone remote, tested internally

Set markers and control transport from an iPhone (iOS 17+) over the local network. Only tested internally. An iPad version is on TestFlight only, and there is no Apple TV version.

Good to know

What you need, and what it does not do.

Status and requirements

  • Mac, macOS 14 or later; the app is sandboxed
  • The actual work needs no account; everything runs locally
  • An email address at first start and an occasional internet connection for the licence check, about every 24 hours. With a cached licence it keeps working offline for around a week
  • Final Cut Pro functions need Final Cut Pro installed
  • Tested internally and by code review only; there is no automated test target for the Mac app yet
  • Planned price: €19. No release date yet

What it does not do

  • No protected screener or DRM mode, no encryption, no anti-recording and no expiry
  • No SharePlay, no roles or multi-user, no project or cloud sync
  • No live bridge or video chat
  • No drawing on the picture and no music cues
  • No Resolve or Avid export; Premiere CSV is the only such format
