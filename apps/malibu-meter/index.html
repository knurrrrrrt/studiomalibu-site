Four steps

From the first start to a checked target.

01

Start the trial

On the first start, enter your email address and click Start Trial. macOS asks for microphone access right away, before you have chosen a source.

02

Choose a source

Right-click the meter window, choose Settings... and open the Input tab. Pick App, Device or Screen Audio. On a fresh install nothing is selected, so the bars stay at silence.

03

Select the audio

App: open Select Process... and pick an app that plays audio; its name appears with a green dot. Device: pick System Default or a listed device. Screen Audio: tick Enable and choose apps in the Applications menu.

04

Read and check

Play audio in the source. The bars show the level. Press Command-Shift-L for the LUFS Meter and pick a preset under Standard: to check the result against a target.

What's inside

What the current version (1.30) actually does.

Measure

Loudness values

Momentary (M), short-term (S) and integrated (I) loudness in LUFS, plus loudness range (LRA) and true peak (TP). Show them as text or as three rings. The loudness meter is off by default: choose LUFS Meter in the View menu. The values are only computed while it is shown.

Three capture modes

App measures one app at a time and needs macOS 14.4 or later. Device measures System Default or a named input device, including virtual devices such as loopback drivers. Screen Audio measures one or more apps chosen from a list, using ScreenCaptureKit.

Surround in Device mode

Sources with 5, 6 or 8 channels (5.0, 5.1, 7.1) are measured for loudness with their real channels; the level bars show a stereo fold-down. Other channel counts, and the App and Screen Audio modes, are measured as stereo.

Level, spectrum, goniometer

Peak and RMS level bars, an optional spectrum with resolutions from 1 to 1/48 octave, and a Lissajous goniometer.

Ballistics and scales

Digital Peak, PPM (EBU/BBC), PPM (DIN), PPM (Nordic) and VU. Scales: Linear (dBFS), Final Cut Pro, DIN, VU (+3), K-20, K-14, K-12 and EBU R128.

Test signal and test tones

Test Signal feeds a built-in sweep through the meters. Export Test Tones… writes five 30-second WAV files with a 997 Hz sine tone, from 0 to -30 dBFS, to load into the app you measure.

Loudness targets

Presets: EBU R128, Netflix, Apple TV+, YouTube, Spotify, Apple Music and ATSC A/85. Each has a target, an allowed deviation and a true-peak limit; EBU R128, for example, is -23 LUFS, ±1 LU and -1 dB. The result counts as passed only when integrated loudness is in range and true peak is at or below the limit. Netflix is selected by default.

Your own status colours

Choose the colours for Too Quiet, In Range and Too Loud. They colour the integrated value and the pass or fail indicator; the true-peak value uses only In Range and Too Loud.

App level calibration

In App mode the level is compensated automatically from your output device. If a test tone does not read its true level, calibrate with Auto-calibrate or by hand.

Auto-Reset on Silence

Resets integrated loudness and range after about 1.5 seconds of silence. It is off by default, like Always on Top.

Settings and gradients

Import and export your settings and custom colour gradients from the File menu. Numeric values from a settings file are limited to valid ranges on import.

Good to know

What you need, what to watch for, and what it does not do.

Requirements

  • macOS 14.0 or later; App mode needs macOS 14.4 or later
  • Microphone access for Device (macOS asks at the first start), audio-capture access for App, Screen Recording access for Screen Audio
  • Free trial after you enter your email address; a license key unlocks the app afterwards
  • The app is sandboxed and needs a network connection for the license check and updates

What it does not do

  • App mode measures one app at a time, as a stereo signal
  • App and Screen Audio never compute loudness from surround channels
  • Surround loudness works only in Device mode with 5, 6 or 8 channels; other counts are measured as stereo
  • Test Signal works only with System Default as input device and is not a calibrated tone

Worth knowing

  • Reset Peaks (Command-R) and a double-click reset only the peak hold, not integrated loudness or range. Those restart when you choose the source again, or with Auto-Reset on Silence.
  • After you switch the output device in App mode, choose the app again so the new compensation applies.
  • Deterministic level (experimental) is off by default. It can leave the meter silent on some Macs; turn it off if the signal drops out.
  • Treat LUFS readings of the exported test tone as approximate.
